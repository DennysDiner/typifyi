# Independent audit: Tasmanian RTI tracker

Audit date: 2026-09-24. The auditor had no part in building the tool. The only file created is this report; no other file was changed.
Commands were run in `tas-rti-tracker/` with `.venv` active. A scratch database was built with `RTI_DATA_DIR=/tmp/auditdata`. Probe scripts (kept outside the repo) replayed the synthetic `FakeSite` from `tests/conftest.py` against the real `Monitor` and `Fetcher`.

**Network:** no `*.tas.gov.au` host, AustLII or archive.org site could be reached. The legal points below come from the files on disk and from **auditor memory**. Every memory point has a confidence figure attached. **No memory point is verified.** The Act must be read before any of them is repeated in a briefing.

**Severity scale**
- **blocker**: the tool must not be used in a briefing until this is fixed.
- **high**: this could produce a false or attackable claim, or leak private data.
- **medium**: a real defect with limited blast radius.
- **low**: hygiene.

Findings are numbered 1 to 64 in section order. Section 7 lists the top 10 to fix first. Section 8 gives the counts.

## 1. Registry completeness (`registry/authorities.yaml`, 184 records)

**What checks out**
- All **29 councils** are present, each as type `council`, status `full`, with a region.
- `rti registry validate` returns OK.
- `rti init` gives 184 authorities and 17 sources. The 17 are 14 disclosure-log sources and 3 adjacent sources, of which Hansard is disabled.
- Every one of the 27 authorities with a `disclosure_log_url` ends up polled, either through its own source or through `shares_source_with` (12 Minister records share one log, and `tas_police` shares with `dpfem`). The exceptions are findings 7 and 8.
- `config/scheduler.yaml` names every body on the brief's Tier 1 list.

**The problem:** naming a body in Tier 1 does not mean anything is polled for it.

**1. [blocker] 10 of the 18 Tier 1 bodies have nothing to poll.**
- Location: `config/scheduler.yaml:7-35`. Records at `registry/authorities.yaml:2194` (justice), `:2407` (launceston_cc), `:4484` (stt).
- `rti health` shows these as `no_log`: `justice`, `building_tas`, `stt`, `tasnetworks`, `tasports`, `tasrail`, `ttline`, `tas_irrigation`, `maib`, `launceston_cc`.
- Only DPAC, Treasury, State Growth (legacy URL), NRE, DPFEM/Police, Health, Hydro and the shared ministerial log are polled.
- The registry itself records evidence that logs exist and were missed:
  - STT's `rti_status_basis` cites ABC 2026-05-20 saying STT already publishes a log.
  - Justice's notes record a "Disclosure Register" published within 48 hours.
- **Fix:** find these URLs before any briefing. Make `rti health` report "Tier 1 with no source" as a FAILURE, not as `no_log`. Until then, do not describe Tier 1 as monitored.

**2. [blocker] The coverage statistic is not supportable.**
- Location: `coverage_report.md:7-11`, generated by `rti_tracker/registry.py:220-221`.
- The report says 144 bodies have "NO discoverable disclosure log", and that "each row is a finding: a body … that publishes no (findable) log".
- Three things undermine that number:
  - Most per-body searches were never run. Record notes say the WebSearch budget was "exhausted after this agent's 51st search".
  - About 34 of the rows are business units of departments (finding 3), not public authorities.
  - At least STT and TasTAFE are reported by the ABC as publishing logs.
- A hostile reader only needs one counter-example to discredit the whole table.
- **Fix:** relabel the column "not yet searched" versus "searched, none found". Exclude via-parent units. Do not quote the number until each row has a recorded, run search.

**3. [high] 34 business units are recorded as standalone public authorities with status `full`.**
- Example: `registry/authorities.yaml:93` (`ambulance_tas`). Its `rti_status_basis` says "not a separate public authority - applications go to the parent department", yet its status is `full`.
- The affected ids are: ambulance_tas, arts_tas, bdm, business_tas, cbos, cisve, climate_change_office, community_corrections, coordinator_general, dss, events_tas, fsst, jobs_tas, land_tasmania, libraries_tas, local_gov_division, mrt, office_aboriginal_affairs, ori, osem, pws, recfit, screen_tas, sentencing_advisory, service_tas, ses, skills_tas, sport_rec_tas, state_planning_office, tas_prison_service, tfs, tis, worksafe.
- `tas_police` also appears on this list, but it is correctly its own limb of the definition.
- `crown_law` duplicates `solicitor_general`, and both sit inside Justice.
- **Fix:** add `rti_status: via_parent` (or type `business_unit`). Exclude these from the coverage and "no log" counts, and roll their releases up to the parent.

**4. [high] The completeness cross-check against the annual report has never been run.**
- Location: `research/annual_report_crosscheck.md` §0 ("Per-authority names … recovered: 0"); `DECISIONS.md` D3.
- The registry is a research seed list. It has not been checked against the list of bodies that actually report.
- **Fix:** run `rti annual import` and `rti annual crosscheck` on 2023-24 and 2024-25 before claiming the registry is complete.

**5. [medium] Bodies missing from the registry (auditor memory, confidence in brackets).**
- **Law Society of Tasmania** (80%). `LEGAL_MODEL.md:56` itself says it is partially covered under s 6, yet there is no record.
- **Marine Farming Planning Review Panel** (55%). Relevant to salmon campaigns.
- **Launceston Flood Authority** (45%). A council authority.
- **Rivers and Water Supply Commission** (40%).
- **Poppy Advisory and Control Board** (45%).
- **Veterinary Board of Tasmania** (45%).
- **Racing Appeals Board** and **Forest Practices Tribunal** (35%).
- **Hydro subsidiaries such as AETV Pty Ltd** (35% that they exist as separate companies). Whether GBE subsidiaries are covered at all is UNCERTAIN.
- **Former Ministers of earlier ministries.** They are needed so that historical entries on the DPAC Ministers' log stay attributable. The auditor cannot name the full list with confidence; check it against DPAC ministry lists.
- **Fix:** add each body as `UNCERTAIN` with the basis "auditor memory", and resolve it through the annual-report cross-check.

**6. [medium] Several statuses are too timid, and a few may be wrong.**
- Location: `registry/authorities.yaml:3531` (`ntdc`), `:830` (`copping_jointauth`), plus dulverton_waste, cradle_coast_authority, southern_tas_regional_waste_authority, rbf_board (`:3922`), teyf, cyso_regulator, talc, tic, energy_ombudsman, parole_board, official_visitors, legal_profession_board (`:2477`).
- **Probably `full`:**
  - The council-owned company and the joint authorities (60%). LEGAL_MODEL §1.1 row 9 marks the "council-owned company" limb as snippet_verified, and joint authorities are established under the Local Government Act.
  - rbf_board, teyf, cyso_regulator (55%).
- **Probably `partial`:**
  - tic and parole_board (judicial or quasi-judicial functions, 45%).
  - energy_ombudsman and official_visitors (s 6-type administration-only, 40%).
- **legal_profession_board** is `full` but may be `partial`, because s 6 carves out complaint information (35%).
- **Fix:** resolve against s 5 and s 6 when the Act is read. Do not present the UNCERTAIN count as a legal finding.

**7. [medium] A defunct department is still polled, and its failures are invisible.**
- Location: `rti_tracker/registry.py:127-143` (a source is created whatever `active` says); `rti_tracker/health.py:261` (`WHERE a.active=1`).
- `communities_tas` (abolished in 2022) has an enabled source, id 1, polled every 6 hours.
- `rti health` omits it, so any errors there never surface.
- **Fix:** skip or disable sources for inactive authorities, and list orphaned enabled sources in the health report.

**8. [medium] `tlgc` and `treasury` both poll the same URL.**
- Location: `registry/authorities.yaml:5317`; sources 12 and 14.
- The effects:
  - Every Treasury release is duplicated under TLGC.
  - Every alert fires twice.
  - The host is hit twice per tick.
  - `documents` rows are created only for whichever item fetches first, because of the per-URL dedupe at `monitor.py:261-265`.
- **Fix:** give `tlgc` the setting `shares_source_with: treasury`.

**9. [medium] Individual Ministers get no releases attributed to them.**
- Location: `scheduler.yaml:27-35`; `registry.py:130`.
- Everything on the DPAC Ministers' log is attributed to `ministers_dpac_log`. The eight "campaign-relevant Minister" Tier 1 entries therefore do nothing, and filtering the feed by `min_abetz` returns nothing.
- **Fix:** parse the Minister's name out of each log entry and attribute it. Keep former Ministers active for historical attribution.

**10. [medium] The two-reading regional design never applies to state bodies.**
- Location: `deadlines.py:134`; every agency, GBE and SOC record has `region: null`.
- **Fix:** set a region, either the head-office location or the applicant's location, or make the reference point an explicit setting per application.

**11. [medium] Machinery-of-government and Minister facts from 2026 rest on search snippets only.**
- Examples: State Growth becoming Building Tasmania on 2026-07-02; Homes Tasmania joining Building Tasmania in December 2026; portfolio moves in June 2026; Howlett and Ogilvie as former Ministers.
- The auditor cannot confirm any of these from memory.
- Misnaming a department or Minister in front of Legislative Council members costs credibility.
- **Fix:** verify each against DPAC and Premier pages, and record the evidence URL.

**12. [low] Name resolution matches loose substrings, so it can hide gaps.**
- Location: `registry.py:177-191`.
- Tests of `resolve_authority`: "Council" → `dorset_c`; "Tasmania" → `arts_tas`; "Premier" → `min_rockliff`.
- The effects:
  - The annual cross-check can "resolve" subtotal rows or badly parsed names, which hides registry gaps.
  - `rti app new --authority` can attach an application to the wrong body.
- **Fix:** accept exact matches and aliases only. Print fuzzy candidates for a human to confirm.

**13. [low] Campaign-relevant bodies sit in Tier 2.**
- These are:
  - `tlgc` (pokies)
  - `fpa` (forestry)
  - `epa` (Nyrstar, Bell Bay)
  - `marinus_link` and `aurora` (energy)
  - `coordinator_general` (industry grants)
  - `mpdc` and `stadiums_tas`
- **Fix:** promote them once they have logs.

**14. [low] Stale sources are never switched off.**
- Location: `registry.py:130-143`.
- Removing a `disclosure_log_url`, or adding `shares_source_with`, leaves the old source enabled.
- **Fix:** disable any source whose URL no longer appears in the registry.

**15. [high] A deemed refusal gets no lapse date for going to the Ombudsman.**
- Location: `rules.yaml:228-236` (`days: null`); `deadlines.py:313-316` ("may apply now").
- The auditor thinks it plausible (50%) that s 45 carries a 20-working-day limit, running from the date the refusal is deemed made (the last day of the decision period).
- This is the applicant's *own* deadline, so the safe default is the earliest plausible date. Showing nothing risks Kurt losing his review right.
- **Fix:** show a precautionary date of 20 working days from the deemed-refusal date, labelled UNCERTAIN, and alert on it.

**16. [high] An unrecorded decision-maker is treated as a delegate.**
- Location: `deadlines.py:249` and `:318`.
- A decision signed by a Secretary or CEO (the principal officer) is then sent down the internal-review route, and no s 45 Ombudsman window is shown. Probe: `decision_notified` with `{}` produced "Apply for internal review".
- **Fix:** make `decision_maker` mandatory. If it is unknown, show both windows and flag the entry.

**17. [medium] Late extension events silently cancel a deemed refusal.**
- Location: `deadlines.py:211-218`.
- The third-party +20 applies whenever the event is recorded, even after the 20-day period has expired. Probe: accepted 3 Aug, consultation decided 5 Sep, today 10 Sep. The tool showed "awaiting decision, due 28 Sep", although the tool's own rule deems the application refused on 31 Aug.
- `extension_agreed` recorded after expiry behaves the same way. Auditor memory (55%): an extension must be made within the period.
- **Fix:** apply extensions only when the event date is on or before the current due date. Otherwise keep the deemed refusal and add a warning.

**18. [medium] The "30 working days after negotiation" alternative is doubtful, and it appears on every application.**
- Location: `rules.yaml:68-82`; `deadlines.py:224-228`.
- The auditor does not recall any 30-day rule (35% that one exists).
- The tool shows it on every application, even when no negotiation happened. There is no event for negotiation at all.
- It also still shows after an agreed extension (probe). A hostile reader will ask what it means.
- **Fix:** add a `negotiation_commenced` event, and show the alternative only when that event exists.

**19. [medium] The CLI never shows the 20-day alternative for internal review.**
- Location: `rules.yaml:175-189`; `deadlines.py:98-107`.
- The auditor favours 15 working days for the internal review decision (60%), so using 15 is right.
- But `Deadline.display()` drops `alternative`, so `rti app status` never shows the 20-day date (see scenario g). The web template does show it.
- **Fix:** print the alternative in `display()`.

**20. [medium] Working days: Easter Tuesday is not modelled.**
- Location: `rules.yaml:15-29`.
- The auditor thinks s 5 defines a working day by reference to Saturdays, Sundays and statutory holidays (55%), so `uncertain: true` is right.
- Easter Tuesday: the auditor thinks (50%) it is a holiday under the *Statutory Holidays Act 2000* for the State Service and some awards only. If so, agency deadlines move by a day, and the engine offers no such reading. In scenario j the date would be **4 May instead of 1 May**.
- The Christmas shutdown is correctly *not* excluded (65%).
- **Fix:** add an "Easter Tuesday (State Service)" alternative reading for Agencies.

**21. [medium] `regional.yaml` has the wrong geography.**
- Location: `legal/holidays/regional.yaml:13-21`.
- Show days are declared for particular municipalities, not for three regions (auditor memory, 70%). The file:
  - applies King Island Show to every north-west council;
  - applies Flinders Island Show to every north council, including Launceston;
  - lists Recreation Day only for the north. In fact it is observed wherever Regatta is not, including the north-west (70%);
  - omits Devonport Cup.
- Probe with `include_unverified_regional=True`, Launceston, accepted 5 Oct: the tool gives 5 Nov; this reading should give 4 Nov (Launceston Show plus Recreation Day). A Burnie council gets no Recreation Day shift at all.
- **Fix:** key holidays by municipality, then map each authority to a municipality.

**22. [medium] The regional reading can never appear, but the wording says it can.**
- Location: `holidays_cal.py:100-103`; `cli.py:258-277` has no flag; `DECISIONS.md:47` and `README.md` refer to a `--include-unverified-regional` flag that does not exist on `rti app status`.
- The status output still says "Deadlines shown as a range where readings differ". For hobart_cc and launceston_cc no range is ever shown.
- Hiding unverified holidays is defensible. Implying that they were considered is not.
- For the *authority's* deadlines (the deemed-refusal date), a public claim should use the *later* date.
- **Fix:** say "regional holidays not applied (unverified)". Add the CLI flag. Use the latest reading for public claims of deemed refusal, and the earliest for Kurt's own windows.

**23. [low] Transfer rule: agreed, and correctly flagged.**
- Location: `rules.yaml:133-146`.
- The auditor agrees with "earlier of the transfer date and 10 working days after receipt" (55%). `uncertain` is correctly set.
- The deemed receipt can fall before the actual transfer. In scenario i it is 17 Aug against a transfer on 20 Aug, and the tool warns about this correctly.

**24. [low] Section numbers are hard-coded in code, contrary to the "no numbers in code" claim.**
- Location: `deadlines.py:248` (`"s 22"`), `:272` (`"s 43"`), `:279` (`"s 47"`); `applications.py:35-39` (`"s 13"`, `"s 16"`).
- The auditor cannot confirm "s 22" as the notice-of-decision section (low confidence either way).
- **Fix:** take these from `rules.yaml`.

**25. [low] Fees: waiver paragraph letters are uncertain in both directions.**
- Location: `rules.yaml:263-299`.
- The auditor thinks (50%) the journalist waiver may be in the original text rather than inserted in 2024. If so, the paragraph letters differ: (c) journalist, (d) public interest.
- **Fix:** do not cite paragraph letters in a briefing. The dollar figures are plausible but unverified.

**26. [low] "Disclosure logs are not statutory" needs softer wording.**
- Location: `rules.yaml:333-348`.
- The auditor agrees there is no mandatory statutory duty (65%). There is low-confidence (35%) recall of "should consider routine disclosure" wording in s 12 or the Ombudsman Manual.

**27. [low] The annual-reporting section contradicts another file and is over-cautious.**
- Location: `rules.yaml:350-358` and `annual_report_crosscheck.md` §2.4.
- `rules.yaml` names the Secretary of Justice as reporter (snippet). The cross-check note names the responsible Minister (memory).
- The note "whether Ministers report separately" is too timid: the 2022-23 sector totals already include 51 applications to Ministers.

**28. [low] "PASSED" is printed on deadlines that were met.**
- Location: `deadlines.py:285-286`.
- In scenario e, "PASSED Decision due 2026-08-31" appears although the decision came on 28 Aug. This is easily misread in a briefing.

**29. [low] `decision_notified` does not say which date it records.**
- s 43 runs from the day the applicant *received* notice. The event can hold either the letter date or the receipt date, and posted letters may carry a deemed-receipt rule.
- **Fix:** record both dates.

**30. [low] Decisions by a Minister's delegate are not modelled.**

## 4. Archive, provenance, fetching and change detection

**Tests:** `pytest -q` gives **46 passed, 1 skipped**. The skip is the recorded-fixture replay, because `tests/fixtures/recorded/` is empty. Every adapter test uses hand-built synthetic pages.

**Test sampling:** `tests/test_monitor.py:27-74` does exercise listing-level new items, edits (with a diff and version rows) and removals (carrying archived document hashes). Document change detection, however, is proven only by calling `fetch_item_documents()` directly (lines 63-73). The comment at line 63 admits that normal polling does not re-fetch. There are no tests for pagination, a 404 listing, a 304 after a failure, the auto adapter switching format, or entries sharing a date.

**31. [blocker] Paginated logs produce false "silent removal" alerts.**
- Location: `adapters/auto.py:42-43`; `html_list.py`/`html_table.py` `next_page`; `monitor.py:61` (`max_pages=25`); `monitor.py:193-200`.
- Without a configured `next_selector`, and every source currently has none, only page 1 is read.
- On a log that shows its latest N entries per page, each new release pushes the oldest entry to page 2. That entry is then recorded as `item_removed`, severity **high**, and an immediate alert fires.
- Logs longer than 25 pages hit the same problem.
- This is a systemic generator of false "silently removed" claims.
- **Fix:** detect pagination links heuristically. Refuse to mark removals when the listing is possibly truncated (items missing only from the tail). Treat a removal as confirmed only after a full crawl.

**32. [blocker] Different releases published on the same date are merged into one item.**
- Location: `adapters/base.py:184` (`_REF_PAT` matches `12/05/2025`); `html_table.py:112,123`; `base.py:197` (`dedupe`).
- Probe: a table with two releases dated 12/05/2025 ("salmon briefings", "Nyrstar correspondence") parsed to **one** item. Its reference became "12/05/2025", the Nyrstar title was lost, and both PDFs were attached to the salmon item.
- A later same-date release will show up as an "edit", not a new release, so no `new_item` alert or watchlist hit fires on its title.
- `html_list.py:52,55` has the same date-as-reference fault.
- **Fix:** reject date-shaped references and require an RTI-style prefix. The stable key must always include title and URL.

**33. [high] A listing that returns 404 or 410 is processed as a success.**
- Location: `fetch.py:182-195` treats only 401, 403, 451, 429 and 5xx as failures; `monitor.py:115`.
- Probe, established log: the listing URL returned 404 with a nav page, giving **3 false removals and 1 junk "new release"** with status `changed`.
- Probe, first poll: a 404 URL gives status `ok`, 0 items, and health `ok` indefinitely.
- Every registry URL is an unverified search-snippet URL, so this will be common at go-live.
- **Fix:** any status other than 200 or 304 is an error. Zero items on a first poll is an error.

**34. [high] Changed documents are never detected in normal operation.**
- Location: `monitor.py:188-189`, `:204-207`.
- Documents are fetched only for new items, or when an item gains new document URLs. A PDF replaced at the same URL, for example re-redacted, is never re-fetched.
- Probe: the bytes of a PDF changed, then `poll(force=True)` ran. Result: `changed_documents=0`.
- Release pages are never re-expanded either.
- `DECISIONS.md` D11 says `--force` catches these cases. That is false.
- **Fix:** add a scheduled document re-check (conditional GET, re-hash) and periodic re-expansion of release pages.

**35. [high] A 304 response hides a parser failure.**
- Location: `monitor.py:219`: the ETag and Last-Modified are saved *before* parsing, in autocommit.
- Probe:
  1. The page changes and parses to 0 items: error.
  2. The next poll gets 304: `not_modified`, `consecutive_failures=0`, `last_status='ok'`.
- The layout break disappears from health until the page next changes. An exception inside the adapter behaves the same way.
- **Fix:** save the ETag only after a successful parse and commit.

**36. [high] The auto adapter re-detects the format on every poll and never saves it.**
- Location: `adapters/auto.py:23-40`; `cli.py:95-102`.
- `config["detected_format"]` is set on an in-memory dict and never written back to `sources.config`, so `rti registry suggest-formats` always prints nothing.
- Worse, the winning parser can switch between polls as the page grows. The `external_key` scheme then changes, which produces mass false removals and mass new items.
- **Fix:** save the detected format, pin it after the first success, and alert on any switch.

**37. [high] "Last seen" is wrong, and removal evidence is not linked.**
- Location: `monitor.py:103-114`; `web/templates/item.html`.
- `items.last_seen_at` is **not updated** on 304 or unchanged polls. So "last seen" is the time of the last listing *change*, possibly weeks before the entry actually disappeared.
- `item_removed` records neither the last capture that still contained the entry nor the first capture without it.
- A removal claim in a briefing therefore cannot be backed by a before/after pair from the tool.
- **Fix:** update `last_seen_at` and a `last_capture_id` on every successful poll. Store `prev_capture_id` and `curr_capture_id` in the removal detail. Show both in the UI and the citation pack.

**38. [high] Provenance is self-attested and not tamper-evident.**
- Location: `migrations/001_init.sql:121` ("Never updated, never deleted" is only a comment); `archive.py:89-92`.
- There are no UPDATE or DELETE triggers on `captures` or `blobs`, no hash chain and no external timestamp. Blob files stay writable.
- `rti archive verify` re-hashes blobs only. It would not notice an edited `retrieved_at`, a changed URL, or a deleted row.
- Under hostile scrutiny, "our database says we fetched it at T" is weak evidence.
- **Fix:**
  - add triggers that abort any UPDATE or DELETE on `captures` and `blobs`;
  - add an append-only hash chain over captures;
  - anchor it daily (RFC 3161 timestamp authority or OpenTimestamps);
  - make blobs read-only (0444);
  - optionally submit key pages to the Wayback Machine.

**39. [medium] Tracking is by title and URL, so edits look like removals.**
- Location: `html_list.py:55`; `html_table.py:123`.
- A retitled entry, or a re-uploaded file with a new URL (Squiz asset URLs change), is reported as a *removal* plus a new item, not an edit.
- **Fix:** pair each removal with a same-day addition by similarity, and label the pair "possible replacement".

**40. [medium] A document that reverts to an earlier version crashes the tick.**
- Location: `001_init.sql:205` (`UNIQUE(url, sha256)`); `monitor.py:260-274`, `:204`.
- Probe: when a document goes A, then B, then back to A, `IntegrityError` is raised. The document loop in `poll()` sits outside any try block, so `run_due` aborts and the remaining sources are not polled.
- **Fix:** handle the revert as its own change type, and catch errors per source.

**41. [medium] A document that later returns 404 is not recorded.**
- Location: `monitor.py:258`.
- Anything other than 200 is skipped silently. Probe: a later 404 on a document recorded no change.
- **Fix:** record a `document_unavailable` change.

**42. [medium] A manually imported annual report is recorded as a download.**
- Location: `cli.py:379`.
- `rti annual import` stores a local PDF as a capture of `--source-url` with `retrieved_at` set to now. That falsely asserts the file was fetched from that URL at that time.
- **Fix:** record `kind='manual_import'` with a `file://` URL, and keep the claimed source URL in a separate field.

**43. [medium] robots.txt handling and politeness gaps.**
- Location: `fetch.py:100-112`, `76-80`, `117`.
- A robots.txt that returns 5xx, or fails at the network level, is treated as "allow all". RFC 9309 says 5xx means disallow everything; this is a way a block can be bypassed.
- On a redirect to another host, robots.txt is checked only for the original URL, not for the destination host.
- The `sources.robots_*` columns are never written.
- `Crawl-delay` and `Retry-After` are ignored.
- **Fix:** follow RFC 9309, re-check robots.txt for the final host, and honour `Retry-After`.

**44. [medium] Rate limits reset with every process, and cron runs can overlap.**
- Location: `fetch.py:43-50`, `119-135`; `deploy/crontab.example:3`.
- Rate-limit and backoff state is held in memory per process. Cron starts a new process every 10 minutes with no lock.
- Runs can overlap, doubling traffic to a host and duplicating alerts.
- **Fix:** wrap the cron job in `flock -n`, and keep per-host state in the database.

**45. [medium] A source that has never worked never raises an "adapter failing" alert.**
- Location: `monitor.py:304-313`.
- The failure start is taken as the *previous attempt* time, so it never reaches 24 hours. Probe: 48 hourly failures, 0 `adapter_failing` changes.
- **Fix:** measure from the first attempt when there has never been a success.

**46. [low] Polling proceeds with a placeholder contact address.**
- Location: `config.py:226-233`.
- If `RTI_CONTACT_EMAIL` is unset, the User-Agent says "set RTI_CONTACT_EMAIL" and polling goes ahead.
- **Fix:** refuse to poll without a contact address.

**47. [low] The PDF-index adapter can attach the wrong document.**
- Location: `adapters/pdf_index.py:74-77`.
- It pairs links to rows by position whenever the counts happen to match.
- **Fix:** match each link to its row by position on the page.

**48. [low] Poor titles, and page furniture picked up as releases.**
- Location: `html_list.py:43`; `base.py:140-142`.
- The title falls back to the longest link text, giving titles like "PDF".
- Footer and sidebar links under `__data/assets` become releases. In the probe, the "RTI application form" was picked up as a release.

**49. [low] Adjacent sources are unmonitored, and the Ombudsman URL disagrees with the research.**
- Location: `health.py:260`; `config/scheduler.yaml:42` against `LEGAL_MODEL.md:253,305`.
- The adjacent sources have a null `authority_id`, so the health report never shows them.
- The Ombudsman URL `/right-to-information/decisions` differs from the snippet-verified `/right-to-information/reasons-for-decisions`. The decisions are split into folders by section, so a single-page adapter would miss most of them.

**50. [low] Only the first page's capture is linked to a snapshot.**
- Location: `monitor.py:123-126`, `221`.
- Captures of pages 2 and later exist but are not linked to their `listing_snapshots` row.

**51. [low] Deadline alerts can be lost, and dates can shift silently.**
- Location: `applications.py:152-158`; `pyproject.toml` (`holidays>=0.60`).
- An alert fires only if a tick runs on exactly the fire date. A missed day loses the alert for good.
- The holiday library is unpinned, so a routine upgrade can silently change computed deadlines, and the engine does not use the holidays stored in the database.
- **Fix:** fire on "due or overdue and not yet sent". Pin `holidays`, or compute from the DB snapshot.

**52. [low] The tests are weaker than their names suggest.**
- Location: `tests/test_deadlines.py:36-60`; `tests/test_monitor.py:63-73`.
- The deadline tests check the engine with the same calendar it uses, which is partly circular.
- The document-change test name overstates what normal polling does.
- **Fix:** add independently computed expected dates and the missing monitor edge cases above.

## 5. Website and privacy

**53. [high] The GitHub Actions workflow would publish Kurt's applications.**
- Location: `deploy/github-actions-poll.yml:35-36`.
- The example workflow commits `data/` and pushes it. That directory holds `rti.db` (applications, events, links) and `data/archive` (attachments such as decision letters).
- The repository is `DennysDiner/typifyi`. If it is public, or ever becomes public, all of that is published.
- **Fix:** never commit the private tables. Split them into a separate, encrypted database, or drop this option.

**54. [high] Datasette does not actually hide the application tables.**
- Location: `rti_tracker/web/datasette_metadata.yaml:18-25`.
- `hidden: true` only removes a table from the index page. The table still loads at `/rti/applications`, and arbitrary SQL is allowed by default.
- Tables that are not hidden at all also leak application details:
  - `alerts`: its payloads carry the authority and reference of Kurt's deadline alerts (`alerts.py:52`);
  - `deadline_alerts_sent`;
  - `captures`: attachment filenames appear as `file://…` with `kind='attachment'`;
  - `blobs`.
- **Fix:** move the private tables into a separate database file. Set Datasette permissions to deny them, with `allow_sql: false`.

**55. [high] Stored XSS reaches the private pages.**
- Location: `web/templates/search.html:4` (`snip|safe`); `web/app.py:118-126`.
- Search snippets are raw text from third-party documents, rendered without escaping.
- `/archive/{sha}` serves captured HTML inline, from the same origin as the application pages.
- A hostile or compromised government page could therefore run script that reads `/applications`.
- **Fix:** escape snippets and then re-insert the `<mark>` highlights. Serve archive files with `Content-Disposition: attachment`, `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox` and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`.

**56. [medium] The web UI's access control is weak.**
- Location: `web/app.py:26-32`, `111-115`, `174-180`.
- Authentication is off unless `RTI_WEB_USER` is set.
- A user name with a missing password accepts an empty password.
- The POST routes have no CSRF protection and there is no Host check. Any web page Kurt visits can POST fake events into his applications, which changes their deadlines. Without auth, DNS rebinding can also read the private pages.
- **Fix:** always require auth, add CSRF tokens, and add `TrustedHostMiddleware`.

**57. [medium] Kurt's attachments sit in the public archive.**
- Location: `applications.py` `add_attachment` (around lines 55-61).
- They go into the shared archive and captures ledger, and `/archive/{sha}` serves them to anyone logged in.
- **Fix:** give attachments a separate store.

**58. [medium] Provenance display is incomplete.**
- Location: `web/templates/item.html`.
- **Shown** for each document: URL, sha256, retrieval time, HTTP status and a link to the archived copy.
- **Not shown:**
  - the `final_url` after redirects (it is queried but not displayed);
  - the listing capture that proves the entry appeared on the log;
  - the capture of the release page;
  - any before/after evidence for a removal.
- Items with no fetched documents show no provenance at all.
- The citation pack also leaves out the listing capture.
- **Fix:** add all of these.

**59. [low] Push alerts can be public.**
- If `RTI_NTFY_URL` points at a public ntfy.sh topic with no token, deadline alerts (authority and reference) are world-readable.
- **Fix:** require a private topic and a token.

**60. [low] Scraped `javascript:` links are rendered as clickable links.**
- Location: `adapters/base.py:134-137`; `item.html`.
- **Fix:** allow only http and https links.

## 6. Coverage gaps the tool would miss

**61. [high] Some log formats cannot be parsed by any adapter.**
- **Spreadsheets and Word files.** A log published as .xlsx or .docx has no adapter. The auto adapter runs the HTML parsers over the bytes and finds nothing.
- **Script-rendered lists.** Nothing is fetched with JavaScript, so these produce nothing or only the first page:
  - SharePoint lists;
  - Power BI or React widgets;
  - "Load more" buttons;
  - AJAX pagers on Drupal Views or OpenCities.
- **Unrecognised document links.** `is_doc_url` (`base.py:140-142`) misses:
  - Kentico `/getmedia/<guid>/<name>.aspx`;
  - CivicPlus `/DocumentCenter/View/<n>`;
  - Google Drive and OneDrive share links;
  - `download.aspx?id=` links whose path lacks `/download`.
- **Logs with no documents.** Some logs list releases but make copies available only on request, so there are no document links. The auto adapter's scoring favours the wrong parser for these.
- **PDF logs with wrapped rows.** Multi-line rows defeat `pdf_index`'s single-line regex.
- **Squiz Matrix pagination.** Asset-listing pagers (`?result_<id>_page=N`) need a per-site `next_selector`; see finding 31.

**62. [medium] Bodies likely to publish logs whose URLs are not in the registry.**
- The auditor is *not* confident of any exact URL, so none is given.
- **STT:** high. The ABC reported on 2026-05-20 that it publishes, and the registry's own basis cites this.
- **TasTAFE:** high. Same ABC report.
- **Department of Justice:** high. The registry's notes record a "Disclosure Register".
- **UTAS:** moderate. Its RTI page under public-reporting, which the registry already holds as `rti_page_url`, probably carries releases.
- **City of Hobart:** low to moderate.
- **TasWater** and **Tasmanian Audit Office:** low.
- **Aurora Energy, Tasracing and Public Trustee:** ABC says they committed to publishing, so watch their RTI pages.

**63. [medium] Release channels that are not disclosure logs are not tracked.**
- Monthly release of final Cabinet decisions from 1 Sep 2026 (`LEGAL_MODEL.md:28,168`). This matters directly to a Legislative Council audience.
- Right to Know (righttoknow.org.au) request threads for Tasmanian bodies.
- Legislative Council GBE Scrutiny Committee and Estimates answers to questions on notice, and tabled papers.
- Hansard, which is stubbed (`DECISIONS.md` D14).
- **Fix:** add these as adjacent sources.

**64. [low] Bot protection shows up as a block.**
- Sites behind JavaScript challenges such as Cloudflare will appear as 403 "blocked". That is correct, and not evaded, but it should be reported as a coverage gap, not as the authority blocking the tool.

1. **Stop false "silent removal" alerts.** Handle pagination, treat non-200 listings as errors, save the auto-detected format, and require before/after capture evidence before any removal is reported (findings 31, 33, 36, 37).
